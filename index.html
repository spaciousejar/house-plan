Sky House: Two-Storey Plan for a Reader of the Sky

Same irregular plot as measured: East 32′  •  South 24′  •  West 32′  •  North 41.5′  •  diagonals 44.1′ and 48.11′, road on the East. The plan is arranged the way an engineer with a telescope and a wall of books would arrange it: measured, unornamented, built around the empty floor and the open sky.

Site plan: plot outline fitted to the measurements (to scale, ft)

North is up, so the road and the main entrance are on the right (East) side. The dashed red lines are the given diagonals (labelled 44.1′ / 48.11′ as measured). The fitted outline gives 42.9′ / 46.8′ for the same corners, 1.2 and 1.3 ft shorter; a site re-survey will correct this. The open-to-sky zone is the rear (west) side, drawn as the terrace on the first-floor plan.

Ground floor: living, kitchen, bedroom, workroom

Entry: porch → living, with the 8′ × 10′ clear floor in view from the door and kept empty. Kitchen, bath and workroom sit west of it; the bedroom and stair hall on the road side. The workroom keeps the 5 ft bench out of the kitchen and out of the bedroom.

First floor: library, study, bedroom, sky terrace

The library sits over the living/dining and uses its two long solid walls (27 ft) for shelves; the study keeps its low window onto the terrace; the bedroom gains the terrace door. The terrace is the only sky access on the plot, so it carries the fixed 4′ × 4′ pad rather than loose furniture.
Concept drawings for a 2-storey house shaped by one brief: work with the hands, read, and watch the sky. First floor: library/lounge on a 27 ft shelf run, study, bedroom with terrace door, dressing, bath, and the sky terrace with its fixed 4′ × 4′ pad.
